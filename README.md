# Project 1: *Manuscript*

Demo/template for our [first projects](https://typography-interaction-2627.github.io/project/1/).

> **Students will choose a seminal design text from [readings.design](https://readings.design), read and respond to it, and typeset their selection and reply together.**

> The goal of this project is to hone your basic skills in typography, focusing on expression, hierarchy, and form appropriate to a work. You will do this through exploration, trial and error, and responding to critical feedback. And then you will execute this typesetting in code, as a web page


> This project explores how written language can be communicated visually through the design of a web-based article. I chose the article The Shape of Words, which explores how familiarity with certain words can affect how quickly people read and recognize them. Through different examples, perspectives, and observations, the article gradually examines how our familiarity with words influences how we process written language.

>I chose this article because I found its central idea particularly interesting, and I felt that it offered many possibilities for visual experimentation through typography and layout. Since the article approaches its main idea from different examples and angles, I divided the content into distinct sections based on these different points of discussion. Each section uses its own visual treatment to reflect the specific idea being presented, while still maintaining a consistent visual language throughout the website.

>Rather than simply presenting the article as text, the project experiments with transforming its content into visual elements. Through typography and layout, the design aims to help readers understand the article’s main ideas through visual experiences. In this way, the project explores how the visual form of words can influence how a message is read, interpreted, and understood.

>When I first started sketching, my designs were more cautious and less experimental. After receiving feedback, I began looking more closely at the article’s content and thinking about how its ideas could be translated into visual elements. This helped me gradually develop the design direction used in the final website. When I started building the HTML, I first created a basic page structure. However, as I began working with CSS, I realized the HTML didn't have enough hierarchy to style certain sentences or words separately. This led me to think more carefully about how HTML elements are nested within one another and how a webpage is built layer by layer, with each element creating a different level of structure and meaning. I repeatedly went back and adjusted the HTML structure as the CSS developed. At the same time, I looked at the structure and layouts of other article based websites for reference, which helped me think about how to organize different parts of the webpage, such as where the footer should go and how to create a clearer hierarchy. Through this process, I also became more aware of how structure, spacing, and typography can work together to create a clearer and more engaging reading experience.

>When I wrote the CSS, I did not organize my styles from broad to specific at the beginning, which resulted in a lot of repeated styling and made my CSS more complicated than it needed to be. After receiving feedback from the professor, I began reorganizing and simplifying my CSS. Through this process, I developed a better understanding of how CSS works as a system, especially how selectors and reusable styles can be organized to avoid unnecessary repetition.

>One of the things I want to remember from this project is how interactive elements can make a website more engaging. I especially enjoyed using elements such as 'details' and 'summary', as well as hover effects. I like how these small interactions allow readers to click, explore, and reveal information instead of simply reading a static page. They made the website feel more playful and interactive while still supporting the content.
